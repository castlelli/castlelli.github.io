Vildagliptin blocks the enzyme DPP-4. In systemic sclerosis, DPP-4 marks the progenitor fibroblasts that become collagen-producing myofibroblasts. At the Cortellis Drug Repositioning Hackathon, a team of five tested whether that overlap justifies a lab experiment.

The evidence came from four public human skin cohorts, single-cell and network analyses, and a pharmacokinetic model of how much drug reaches skin. Some layers supported the idea and some narrowed it: DPP4 is not globally increased and is not a severity marker. The verdict was to advance to a proof-of-mechanism experiment, which was designed but has not been run.

I co-led the computational analysis and presented the case. The project page re-plots every result as an interactive chart, and each of its 54 references is checked against PubMed.
